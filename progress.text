Step 1 — Explored the initial Seurat dataset (01)
Loaded the initial ICI/cRA Seurat object and examined its structure, cell information, and available metadata.
Step 2 — Explored the original 62 immune gene modules (02)
Examined the curated module library, including module names, gene lists, biological descriptions, and references.
Step 3 — Checked missing module genes (03)
Compared the 62 modules with genes available in the initial ICI/cRA dataset and identified missing genes, including ITGAE, CASP1, and TRAV1-2.
Step 4 — Investigated gene overlap between modules (04)
Identified 116 overlapping module pairs involving 102 unique shared genes, highlighting potential redundancy between modules.
Step 5 — Explored separating overlapping genes (05)
Experimentally removed shared genes from modules and created separate single-gene modules; this was an exploratory analysis, not a change to the original library.
Step 6 — Explored the WuImmune dataset (06)
Loaded and examined the WuImmune immune-cell dataset containing 50,063 cells, 29,733 genes, and samples from 26 patients.
Step 7 — Reclustered WuImmune cells (07)
Performed normalization, variable-feature selection, PCA, neighborhood detection, clustering, and UMAP visualization, obtaining 27 clusters while preserving the original annotations.
Step 8 — Checked module gene availability in WuImmune (08)
Compared all 62 modules against WuImmune genes: 60 had complete coverage, while two had missing genes. All 62 modules remained usable.
Step 9 — Inspected the original module structure (09)
Verified that the original Excel file contained 62 modules and four columns, and confirmed that the WuImmune-compatible module list contained 62 named gene vectors.
Step 10 — Separated identity and functional-state modules (10)
Provisionally classified the original 62 modules into 17 cell-identity modules and 45 functional-state modules, saving two separate Excel files while preserving the original schema.
